A warning while it still matters

RAID 5, RAID 50 and RAID-Z1 on drives of 8 TB or more warn that a rebuild can take days and that a second failure during it loses the array. When the groups are wide enough to move up, one tap switches to RAID 6, RAID 60 or RAID-Z2.

Questions

What is SHR?

Synology Hybrid RAID builds RAID in layers of matching capacity, so mixed drive sizes waste less space than classic RAID. The app supports SHR and SHR-2, and lets you compare them with classic RAID on the same drives.

Which systems does it cover?

On identical drives: RAID 0, 1, 5, 6, 10, 50 and 60, JBOD, and ZFS RAID‑Z1, Z2 and Z3. With mixed drives, bay by bay: Synology SHR and SHR‑2, Unraid, ZFS, SnapRAID and Btrfs RAID1.

Why does my NAS show less than I expected?

Drives are sold in TB and most NAS systems report TiB. RAID Calculator shows both, so the number on the page matches the number you see after setup. For ZFS it also estimates what ZFS itself will report after padding and reserved space, and labels it an estimate.

What doesn’t it cover?

Synology expansion units, SSD cache, and Unraid’s separate pools outside its main array aren’t covered. It doesn’t estimate cost, power or IOPS, and it never touches your actual disks.

Are the speed ratings benchmarks?

No. Speed and availability are 1–5 star comparisons between RAID levels, labeled as such in the app. Capacity figures use standard simplified formulas and don’t account for a specific filesystem’s overhead, apart from the labeled ZFS estimate. NAS systems get no stars at all, since their speed depends on how each one is set up.
